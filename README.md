# SignBridge
### Connecting Deaf individuals with sign language interpreters

SignBridge is an accessibility-focused MVP designed to help Deaf individuals discover sign language interpreters and request communication support. The initial use case focuses on scheduled hospital appointments, with a broader vision of supporting communication across other everyday settings.

## The problem

Communication access is essential for equal participation. The **Rights of Persons with Disabilities (RPwD) Act, 2016** recognises a broad definition of communication that includes signs, sign language, written and visual communication, accessible multimedia and accessible information and communication technology. The Act also addresses accessibility standards and access to information and communication technology (Sections 40 and 42).

However, having accessibility provisions in law does not mean that an interpreter is readily available whenever a Deaf person needs one.

Communication barriers can arise in many settings:

- **Hospitals:** Explaining symptoms, understanding diagnoses, discussing treatment and asking questions.
- **Courts:** Following proceedings and communicating with legal professionals.
- **Offices and service centres:** Accessing public or private services and completing procedures.
- **Public places and events:** Participating in concerts, cultural events and other public experiences.
- **Travel:** Communicating at airports, railway stations, hotels and other transit points.

Finding an interpreter who is suitable for a particular context, location and time can be difficult. SignBridge explores how a single, accessible connection point could make that process easier.

## The vision

**One unified place where Deaf individuals and sign language interpreters can find each other and connect.**

The long-term vision is to support multiple contexts—including healthcare, legal services, workplaces, public events and travel—rather than creating a separate discovery process for every setting.

## Why start with hospitals?

The broader vision is intentionally narrowed for the first version. Hospitals are the starting point because communication gaps in healthcare can have serious consequences for a person's ability to explain symptoms, understand medical information and participate in treatment decisions.

The initial hospital use case also gives the MVP a defined journey: a patient needs an interpreter for a scheduled appointment at a particular place and time. This makes it possible to focus on a manageable core workflow before expanding into more varied settings.

This is a product-scope decision, not a claim that healthcare is simple. Emergency interpretation, confidentiality, interpreter availability and clinical communication all introduce additional complexity beyond this MVP.

## MVP features

The current prototype demonstrates the core connection and booking workflow:

1. **Interpreter discovery** – browse available interpreter profiles.
2. **Profile viewing** – review information about an interpreter.
3. **Booking request** – submit a request for an appointment.
4. **Interpreter response** – accept or decline a booking request through the demo interpreter portal.
5. **Booking status** – reflect whether a request is pending, confirmed or declined.

## Current prototype limitations

- The interpreter response workflow is demonstrated through a **demo interpreter portal within the patient-facing experience**; it is not a separate production interpreter account or login.
- The prototype demonstrates a booking workflow. It does not establish real-time interpreter availability, verified credentials, hospital partnerships or integration with hospital systems.
- It is an MVP demonstration, not a production-ready healthcare service.

## Product thinking

The project follows a focused MVP approach:

- Start with a meaningful, clearly defined communication problem.
- Choose one initial context rather than attempting to serve every use case at once.
- Build and demonstrate the smallest end-to-end workflow that connects the two sides of the platform.
- Keep the broader multi-context vision as a potential direction for future development.
